# Researching longer-term holds

Use this method when the user wants sustained value beyond a short stream. Rank the opportunity and its durability, then assess the production it can support in the user's scoring. Extra minutes alone do not establish a valuable pickup; low usage, limited peripheral production or poor efficiency can leave a player unhelpful even in a large role.

## Find candidates beyond the projection leaders

Start with the full live FREEAGENT and WAIVERS pool. The collector's default top 35 and season projections help organize the search but can miss newly promoted starters or favor veterans whose roles have disappeared. Cross-check recent available-player performances and significant teammate absences to surface lower-projected candidates. Verify current NBA membership and fantasy ownership before treating an appealing player as obtainable.

Batch independent ESPN athlete-overview requests for the shortlist and relevant injured or returning competitors. Extract compact fields: player ID/name, availability, headline, story and publication date. Save useful raw responses locally, but avoid printing entire news arrays, which include large amounts of unrelated metadata. A stale latest article is not evidence of the player's present role; use current box scores to fill the gap. Use external reporting for material gaps or an explicit research request.

## Test the role against actual games

Inspect several recent box scores, including games before and after the role-changing event where possible. Compare starts, minutes, shot attempts, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks and turnovers; calculate actual fantasy points using refreshed league weights. Inspect closing lineups when available and consequential. Separate a poor shooting night with retained minutes from a loss of rotation time, and account for blowouts, foul trouble, ejections and temporary rest absences.

Trace who actually received the missing minutes. Check whether a candidate already started while the competing teammate was available: that supports a stronger case than assuming an injury created the starting job. Conversely, a projected replacement who remains a DNP or plays very little is not an add solely because teammates are out.

For each leading candidate, explain:

- **Duration:** a permanent rotation change, a long injury absence, a brief absence or an uncertain opportunity. Reevaluation dates are not return dates.
- **Competition:** other beneficiaries, returning teammates and plausible reduced-minute scenarios. An absent starter does not give all their minutes or usage to one replacement.
- **Production:** which repeatable contributions fit this points league; distinguish actual recent fantasy points from preseason baselines and estimates.
- **Failure condition:** the role or health news that would weaken the hold. State confidence without presenting a two-game sample as an established average.

## Compare acquisition and roster costs

Refresh the potential drop's role too. A replacement player losing minutes when a starter returns may be a better drop than a developing core player with one poor game. For injured stashes, weigh likely absence and uncertain ramp-up against occupied IR space, existing injuries and lost usable starts. A famous name or high stale projection does not compensate for months without a credible return path.

Keep long-term ranking separate from waiver timing and immediate schedule value. Read current ownership, waiver status and the scheduled processing timestamp in Pacific time; a past processing date does not prove clearance or an awarded claim. Report unresolved status explicitly. Compare remaining games within the verified matchup boundaries, opponents, tip times and legal active slots after the expected acquisition date. For a schedule tie, say so rather than inventing a schedule advantage; opponent quality, role and long-term value can still break the decision tie.

## Present and preserve the judgment

Lead with a preferred add/drop, then distinguish a safer producer, an upside hold and a conditional stash when those alternatives exist. Give the role evidence, availability, main risk and what would change the ranking in compact phone-friendly bullets. Explain major rejections when they prevent misleading projection-led choices. Save dated source links/times and conclusions beside the research packet, with action state explicitly research-only. Do not preserve today's player ranking as a standing skill recommendation.
